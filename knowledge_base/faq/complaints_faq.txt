COMPLAINTS FAQ (Citizen Help)

HIGH priority complaints have an SLA of 4 days.
CRITICAL priority complaints have an SLA of 2 days.
MEDIUM priority complaints have an SLA of 7 days.
LOW priority complaints have an SLA of 14 days.

1) What is a complaint?
A complaint is a formal request raised by a citizen to report a public issue such as poor road conditions, water supply failure, sanitation problems, electricity issues, healthcare concerns, or safety threats. The complaint is tracked with a unique complaint ID.

2) How do I file a complaint?
You can file a complaint by providing a title and a description. You may also upload proof such as images, documents, or a voice note. Complaints can be submitted in multiple languages.

3) What happens after I submit a complaint?
After submission, the system uses AI to:
- Detect the language
- Analyze urgency and sentiment
- Classify the category (e.g., Health, Water, Infrastructure)
- Auto-assign it to the relevant department
- Estimate resolution timeline (SLA and predicted resolution time)

4) How long does it take to resolve a complaint?
Resolution time depends on priority:
- CRITICAL: typically 2 days
- HIGH: typically 4 days
- MEDIUM: typically 7 days
- LOW: typically 14 days
These are SLA targets know as Service Level Agreements.

5) What documents or proofs should I attach?
You should attach proof when available, such as:
- Photos or videos (e.g., broken road, garbage overflow)
- Bills or reports (e.g., electricity damage proof)
- Medical documents (e.g., hospital report)
- Voice notes for quick reporting
Providing proof helps the department verify faster.

6) Can I track my complaint?
Yes. You can view your complaint dashboard to track:
- Status (Submitted, In Progress, Resolved, Closed)
- Assigned department
- Priority and urgency
- Updates from department officers

7) What is SLA?
SLA stands for Service Level Agreement. It defines the maximum expected timeline in which a complaint should be acted upon or resolved based on its priority.

8) When should I mark a complaint as CRITICAL?
A complaint should be CRITICAL if it involves:
- Threat to life or safety
- Serious medical emergency
- Fire, accident, or severe risk
- Crimes, harassment, or violence
- Major public hazards
